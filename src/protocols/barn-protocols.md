# 🚨 Colic {#colic}

_Colic means abdominal pain, not one disease. It ranges from mild gas to surgical emergency. Early action matters._

## Recognize It

*   Flehmen/lip curl, looking at flanks, pawing, kicking at belly
*   Restless, stretching as if to urinate, lying down and getting up repeatedly
*   Rolling (especially violent/repeated rolling)
*   Sweating, bloated abdomen, off feed, reduced or no manure, small dry fecal balls, or diarrhea
*   Mild cases may just look "not right," quiet, or off feed

## Immediate Steps

1. **Call the vet.** Don't wait to see if it gets worse. A mild episode can escalate.
2. **Remove all feed.** Leave water available unless the vet says otherwise.
3. **Take vitals and write them down with the time:**
    *   Heart rate (normal: 28-44 bpm)
    *   Respiratory rate (normal: 8-16 breaths/min)
    *   Temperature (normal: 99-101.5°F)
    *   Gum color and capillary refill time (should be pink, CRT under 2 seconds)
    *   Gut sounds in all four quadrants (should hear 1-3 sounds/min per quadrant)
4. **Hand-walk quietly** in short intervals to prevent violent rolling and injury. Stop if the horse is exhausted, unsafe, or excessively sweaty. A violently painful horse may be safer in a well-bedded stall under observation.
5. **Give** Banamine (Flunixin Meglumine) Paste ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnvw](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnvw)) if directed by vet or per your standing protocol:
    *   Dose: 0.5 mg/lb, once daily
    *   Onset: roughly 15-30 minutes
    *   Duration: 24-36 hours
    *   **Do not re-dose** without vet instruction. Do not stack NSAIDs.
6. **Note:** last feed, last water, last manure, last urination, any recent diet/routine changes, exact medication dose and time.

> **Banamine warning:** Never give injectable Banamine IM (causes severe injection-site reactions). Only IV under vet direction, or use oral paste. Banamine can mask pain from a surgical lesion. If pain returns after expected onset, that is an escalation sign, not reassurance.

## Escalation Ladder

**Mild:** Off feed, subtle depression, reduced manure, occasional flank look, normal vitals → Call vet, remove feed, record vitals, monitor closely.

**Moderate:** Repeated pawing/flank watching, lying down, rolling intermittently, sweating, decreased gut sounds, HR above 50 → Vet exam should be arranged promptly.

**Severe / Emergency:** Violent or repeated rolling, continuous pain, abdominal distension, HR persistently 60+, abnormal gums/CRT, weakness, no manure, or pain breaking through Banamine → Emergency vet and referral discussion immediately.

## Do NOT

*   Wait hours to call because the horse is "only mildly uncomfortable"
*   Force feed or water
*   Give mineral oil, laxatives, or oral fluids without vet instruction
*   Pass a stomach tube or insert anything rectally
*   Repeatedly dose Banamine or combine NSAIDs
*   Walk a painful horse to exhaustion or put handlers in danger

## Types of Colic (Overview)

*   **Gas/spasmodic:** Distension or intestinal spasm. Pain may be intermittent, gut sounds often increased. May respond to vet antispasmodics.
*   **Impaction:** Dry ingesta obstructs the large colon/cecum. Signs may start mild. May need IV/oral fluids via vet nasogastric tube.
*   **Displacement/strangulation:** Bowel moves or twists, compromising passage and blood supply. Pain often severe, HR rises, perfusion worsens. Urgent surgery may be needed. You cannot reliably distinguish this from gas or impaction by signs alone.

# 🚨 Choke (Esophageal Obstruction) {#choke}

_Choke in horses is an esophageal blockage, not a tracheal one. The horse can still breathe, but the esophagus is blocked. Always call the vet._

## Recognize It

*   Feed material, froth, or saliva draining from one or both nostrils
*   Repeated swallowing attempts, retching, coughing, drooling
*   Anxious behavior, neck stretching/arching
*   Visible or palpable swelling/bulge on the left side of the neck
*   Refusal to eat
*   Occasionally colic-like pawing or discomfort

## Immediate Steps

1. **Call the vet immediately.** Even episodes that seem to self-resolve can cause aspiration, esophageal ulceration, or later stricture.
2. **Remove ALL feed, hay, water, and edible bedding.** Move to a clean, non-bedded stall or dry lot. Use a muzzle if needed.
3. **Keep the horse quiet.** Hand-walking may prevent eating or rolling.
4. **Allow the head to stay lowered** and neck extended if the horse is comfortable. This helps reduce aspiration risk.
5. **Gentle neck massage only** if there is an obvious, non-painful bulge. Stop immediately if there is resistance, pain, or agitation.

> **Do NOT:** Put a garden hose in the mouth. Syringe, drench, or force water/oil/electrolytes/medication. Attempt to pass a nasogastric tube. Force the horse's head upward. Repeatedly manipulate the neck. AAEP specifically warns that a hose increases aspiration pneumonia risk. Merck warns that mineral oil aspiration can be fatal.

## What the Vet Will Do

*   Sedate (alpha-2 agonist) and allow spontaneous relaxation/resolution
*   Pass a nasogastric tube and perform gentle lavage with small water volumes
*   IV fluids if dehydrated
*   Evaluate lungs for aspiration pneumonia (ultrasound and other diagnostics)
*   May use antibiotics, anti-inflammatories, tetanus prophylaxis, and mucosal protectants (sucralfate)

## After the Obstruction Clears

*   **Do not immediately resume normal dry feed**
*   Follow the vet's refeeding plan: typically starts with soupy complete-feed mash or soaked pellets, then gradually returns to forage over several days
*   **Monitor for 3-5 days:** temperature, respiratory rate/effort, cough, nasal discharge, depression, appetite, and recurrence of difficulty swallowing
*   Fever over ~101.5°F, cough, increased effort, or discharge warrants reassessment
*   Arrange a dental exam, especially for older horses or recurrent choke

## Prevention

*   Regular dental exams and correction
*   Soak beet pulp, hay cubes, pellets, and feed that expands
*   Slow bolters with feeding modifications and multiple small meals
*   Ensure water access; avoid feeding dry concentrates after work or sedation
*   Dr. Erin's guidance for Mickey: hay can remain available for chew time as long as she is not having choking episodes with hay. If long-stem forage becomes unsafe, use soaked forage pellets/complete feed. Keep pellet feedings to 5 lb or less, multiple feedings per day.

# 😖 Skin Itchiness, Hives & Allergic Reactions {#itch}

_Progressive protocol: start mild, escalate over days if no improvement. Most itch/allergy is not an emergency, but facial/airway swelling is._

## Recognize It

*   Raised wheals or bumps (hives/urticaria), hair standing up over them
*   Rubbing, scratching, biting at skin
*   Tiny bumps from bug bites, scattered welts, barrel/hindquarter rash
*   Swelling of eyelids, lips, muzzle (angioedema, more concerning)
*   **Emergency signs:** noisy or difficult breathing, nostril flare, extended neck, pale/blue gums, weakness, collapse, rapidly progressive facial/throat swelling

> **If airway involvement or systemic signs:** Call vet immediately. This is anaphylaxis territory.

## Immediate Steps

1. **Remove the suspected trigger:** stop any newly started product, move away from insects/dust/spray
2. Keep the horse quiet, shaded, under observation. Do not exercise.
3. Take and record vitals. Photograph lesions with a time stamp.
4. **Do not** add more sprays, oils, or topicals until the trigger is clearer. A cool-water rinse may help remove a surface irritant.

## Treatment Ladder

### Step 1: Antihistamines + Topicals (Start Here)

_Begin at the first sign of itchiness. Don't wait until it's severe._

**Oral antihistamines** (pick one, run for at least 14 days before judging):

*   Cetirizine Hydrochloride 10mg ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnzp](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnzp)): 10 tablets twice daily. If effective, try 15 tablets once daily. Less sedating. Dr. Erin's preferred first line.
*   Hydroxyzine Hydrochloride 50mg ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnzr](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnzr)): 10 tablets twice daily, then 15 once daily if working. Try if cetirizine doesn't work for that horse. Mild sedative effect.

> Dr. Erin: _"For the itchiness, systemic antihistamines like cetirizine or hydroxyzine can be helpful... as we will want to be cautious with systemic steroid administration."_

**Topical relief** (use alongside antihistamines):

*   EquiShield IR (Itch Relief) Spray ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knpea](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knpea)): pramoxine + hydrocortisone + colloidal oatmeal. Shake well, part hair to contact skin, may use daily. **Verdict: Proven.**
*   EquiShield IR (Itch Relief) Shampoo ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knp77](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knp77)): lather, leave 5-10 min, rinse.
*   EQyss Micro-Tek Equine Spray ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnx4](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnx4)): leave-on, apply to skin through parted hair, 2-3x daily.
*   Coat Defense ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knp5u](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knp5u)): preventive powder for sweat itch and bug-bite bumps.

**Wait 7-14 days.** If improving, continue. If no improvement, go to Step 2.

### Step 2: Oral Dexamethasone Taper

_For when antihistamines and topicals aren't enough. Use with caution in metabolic horses (laminitis risk)._

Dex (Dexamethasone Tablets) ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnyn](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnyn)): 8-day taper:

| Day | Tablets/Day |
| --- | --- |
| 1-2 | 4 |
| 3-4 | 3 |
| 5-6 | 2 |
| 7-8 | 1 |

**Total: 20 tablets over 8 days.** Continue antihistamines through and after the taper for maintenance.

**Steroid caution:** Dex carries laminitis and immune-suppression risk, especially for metabolic/overweight horses. Dr. Jon chose Dex rather than Vetalog for Ulyssa because of her weight. Try antihistamines first.

### Step 3: Injectable Steroid (Vet Only)

_For severe allergic reactions or flares unresponsive to oral Dex._

*   Dex (Dexamethasone Injectable) ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnxw](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnxw)): IV use only, per vet direction. Rapid onset. Follow with oral cetirizine/hydroxyzine for maintenance.
*   Vetalog Parenteral (Triamcinolone Acetonide) ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knp00](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knp00)): vet-administered only. Reduce dose for metabolic-risk horses (Avelin received a reduced dose, Dahlia received full dose on 7/16/2026). Laminitis risk emphasized.

### Step 4: Call the Vet

Call if:

*   Widespread or worsening hives despite Step 1-2
*   Facial/eyelid/muzzle swelling
*   Any breathing changes
*   Fever, appetite loss, or behavior change
*   Hives recurring after Dex taper completes

# 🫁 Coughing & Respiratory {#cough}

_Coughing has many causes. Environmental management is the foundation. Don't start steroids without vet direction because infectious causes get worse with steroids._

## Recognize the Pattern

*   **Occasional cough at start of exercise:** may clear mucus, but repeated/persistent coughing is not normal
*   **Dry harsh cough:** could be influenza, environmental irritation, or asthma
*   **Wet/productive cough + nasal discharge:** raises concern for infection, pneumonia, aspiration
*   **Cough while eating hay or entering dusty barn:** airway irritation or asthma
*   **Cough throughout exercise, poor recovery:** warrants vet work-up
*   **At-rest cough with increased respiratory effort:** concerning for severe asthma, pneumonia, or pleural disease

**Observe:** nostril flare, abdominal effort/heave line, respiratory rate, wheezing, tracheal rattle, nasal discharge character, appetite, attitude, fever.

## Immediate Steps

1. **Stop exercise.** Do not work a coughing or labored horse.
2. Take vitals: temp, HR, RR, gum color, respiratory effort.
3. **If fever, discharge, recent travel/exposure, or multiple horses coughing:** isolate and institute biosecurity pending diagnosis (AAEP guideline).
4. Move to the cleanest, best-ventilated environment available, but avoid stressful movement if breathing is difficult.
5. Do not start antibiotics, steroids, or bronchodilators without vet direction.

## When to Call

**Immediately:** Breathing difficulty at rest, marked nostril flare/abdominal effort, blue/pale gums, collapse, suspected aspiration, or rapid deterioration.

**Same day:** Fever, cough with lethargy/anorexia, yellow/green or copious discharge, persistent cough, abnormal lung sounds, increased resting RR, recent transport/exposure, or multiple coughing horses.

**Scheduled work-up:** Cough persisting over days, recurrent seasonal cough, exercise intolerance, cough throughout exercise, or cough recurring despite environmental correction.

## Environmental Management (Do This First, Always)

*   Maximize turnout and fresh air (unless pasture pollen is the trigger)
*   Remove the horse during mucking, sweeping, blowing, and feeding
*   **Do not use leaf blowers around horses**
*   Improve continuous ventilation; don't store hay over stalls
*   Use low-dust bedding (straw is commonly problematic for asthma-prone horses)
*   **Soak hay by fully immersing it for 15-30 minutes.** Merely spraying is not enough. Discard dried hay between meals.
*   Avoid dusty round-bale feeding
*   For severe cases: consider haylage, steamed hay (requires proper high-temp equipment), or complete pelleted forage under vet/nutritionist guidance

## Treatment Ladder (Vet-Directed)

### Step 1: Environmental + Monitoring

Clean up the environment first. Many coughs resolve with dust control alone. Track cough frequency, timing, and triggers for 5-7 days.

### Step 2: Vet Exam + Diagnosis

If environmental changes don't resolve it, get a proper diagnosis. The vet may auscultate, scope, or do bloodwork. Cough can be allergic, infectious, or structural, and the treatment differs dramatically.

### Step 3: Oral Dexamethasone Taper (For Confirmed Allergic/Asthma)

Dex (Dexamethasone Tablets) ([https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnyn](https://app.clickup.com/t/86e2knnyn)): same 8-day taper as allergy protocol:

| Day | Tablets/Day |
| --- | --- |
| 1-2 | 4 |
| 3-4 | 3 |
| 5-6 | 2 |
| 7-8 | 1 |

**Total: 20 tablets over 8 days.** Same metabolic/laminitis cautions apply.

**Critical:** Do NOT use steroids if the cough could be infectious. Steroids suppress the immune system and will make an infection worse. Get the vet to rule out infection first.

### Step 4: Ongoing Management

Seasonal/allergic cough horses may need:

*   Long-term environmental control
*   Early-season antihistamine start (cetirizine or hydroxyzine)
*   Repeat Dex tapers during flare-ups under vet guidance
*   Inhaled bronchodilators (vet-prescribed) for severe asthma
